Six tropes you’ll
see everywhere.

You’ll see these six everywhere in romantasy. Here’s what people love about each one, plus the part that may not work for you right now.

01

They start on opposite sides

Enemies to lovers

They have every reason to dislike—or even destroy—each other. Falling in love changes the fight.

You’ll love it for
Sharp banter and grudging protection
Maybe skip it if
Cruelty between the leads isn’t fun for you

02

The wait is the point

Slow burn

Nothing happens quickly, which is exactly the point. A glance can carry a whole chapter’s worth of tension.

You’ll love it for
Yearning and tiny moments that mean everything
Maybe skip it if
You need the romance to move quickly

03

Destiny gets involved

Fated mates

Magic says they belong together. The interesting part is whether they agree.

You’ll love it for
Big feelings and an impossible-to-ignore bond
Maybe skip it if
You want the relationship to be entirely their choice

04

There’s nowhere to hide

Forced proximity

A journey, a locked room, or a shared mission keeps putting them in each other’s space.

You’ll love it for
Close quarters, private truths, and built-in tension
Maybe skip it if
You prefer a slower, roomier courtship

One of them has walls. The other keeps showing up with enough warmth to get through them.

8 common mix-ups

Similar setup. Different feeling.

Move through the comparisons
01

Enemies to lovers

They begin on opposite sides, with real reasons not to trust each other.

vs.
02

Rivals to lovers

They want the same thing. Competition—not hatred—is what keeps them sparring.

03

Fated mates

Magic, biology, or destiny ties them together before either one has a say.

vs.
04

Soulmates

They fit each other deeply, but fate doesn’t have to be involved.

05

Slow burn

The attraction builds for a long time before anyone finally acts on it.

vs.
06

Insta-love

The connection is immediate. The story begins after the spark, not before it.

07

Forced proximity

A trip, mission, storm, or other situation keeps them together.

vs.
08

Only one bed

It’s one particular moment: two people, one bed, nowhere else to sleep.

09

Marriage of convenience

They choose a practical marriage because it solves a problem for both of them.

vs.
10

Arranged marriage

Their families, duty, or politics choose the match first.

11

Villain romance

The love interest is truly the villain—or remains one while the romance unfolds.

vs.
12

Morally grey love interest

They cross lines, but the story still gives them loyalties, limits, or reasons.

13

Forbidden love

The relationship itself breaks a rule set by family, society, magic, or duty.

vs.
14

Secret relationship

They are already together; the tension comes from keeping everyone else from knowing.

15

Love triangle

Someone has to choose, and that choice is part of the central romantic tension.

vs.
16

Why choose

The story does not require one final choice. The relationships can continue together.

A little context

Why readers search
by trope.

Sometimes genre is too broad. “Romantasy” can mean a tender courtship wrapped in magic, or two enemies trying not to kill each other while a kingdom burns. Tropes give you a better clue about the part you’ll actually spend the book feeling.

They also work best in company. Enemies to lovers feels very different with a slow burn than it does with forced proximity. Fated mates can feel sweeping and romantic, or tense and complicated when destiny gets in the way of choice. The combination matters more than any single label.
